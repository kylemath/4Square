Level guide

All 52 levels, in play order

4square's levels are not random puzzles. Apart from the first warm-ups, every map comes from research on the Four Colour Theorem: exhaustive searches through all sphere maps of up to 34 regions, the hardest starting colourings those searches found, and the pieces a 2026 computer proof search got stuck on. Each level's par is the exact fewest number of swaps, found by exploring every colouring of its map.

The descriptions below are the in-game introductions. The numbers under each title give the map's size and the par. If a level is new to you, read the rules first.

How the islands build up

Splash Cove, Warm-up Beach and Tutorial Town
Small maps that teach tracing and swapping, ending with the icosahedron, the smallest map where every region has five neighbours.
Lock Harbour
Locked chains, Kempe's double swap of 1879 and the counter-example idea Heawood found in 1890.
Far Chains Forest and π Loop Lighthouse
Levels where the winning move is far from the hole, and where the forced chain π leads round in a closed loop of 20 swaps.
Six-Ring Reef, The Depths and Volcano Boss
Holes ringed by regions that each touch six others, and the deepest starting colourings the searches found: up to six swaps from a solution.
Census Frontier and The Abyss
Record holders from complete searches of maps with 33 and 34 regions.
Hurdle Cape, Wrapped Reef and Last Hurdle
Maps built round the hard pieces of an October 2026 proof search, and earlier pits wrapped in extra bands of regions that add decoy chains without making the way out any shorter.

🐚 Splash Cove

−1.1Splash!

7 regions · par 1 swap

Welcome! The grey ? tile in the middle wants an element, but its ring holds fire AND water. Drag from tile to tile along a little group of fire and water until every tile glows, then press SWAP. Make the ring all one element!

−1.2One of each

10 regions · par 1 swap

Now there are three elements, and the ring round the ? has one of each. Catch any chain that touches the ring and swap it: the ring keeps only two elements, and the ? can take the third.

−1.3Forked path

15 regions · par 1 swap

Fire is on the ring twice, water and air only once: swap water or air away. Some chains fork. Trace every arm until the whole chain glows, then swap it.

−1.4First lock

13 regions · par 1 swap · locks

A chain that joins ring tiles of both its elements is a lock (🔒): swapping it just shuffles the ring. Skip the big locks and find a small chain that clears an element.

🏖️ Warm-up Beach

0.1Spark

9 regions · par 1 swap · locks

The silver platform in the middle has no element yet, because its five rim tiles hold all four. Drag across two neighbouring tiles of different elements to trace their chain, then tap it to swap the two elements. Leave the rim with only three.

0.2Ripples

13 regions · par 1 swap · locks

The silver platform in the middle has no element yet, because its five rim tiles hold all four. A few more tiles. Look for the rim element that appears only once, and swap it away.

0.3Archipelago

18 regions · par 1 swap · locks

The silver platform in the middle has no element yet, because its five rim tiles hold all four. Bigger island, same trick: one swap clears an element off the rim. Trace the whole chain before you flip it.

0.4Big water

34 regions · par 1 swap · locks

The silver platform in the middle has no element yet, because its five rim tiles hold all four. The biggest island in the game, but still one swap. Chains can run far across the island; follow the bridges.

🏘️ Tutorial Town

1First flip

12 regions · par 1 swap · locks

The real tutorial. Trace a chain: drag (or tap tile by tile) across neighbours that alternate two elements, until every tile of that chain glows. Then tap the chain to swap its two elements. Clear one element off the rim.

2Open doors

22 regions · par 1 swap · locks

A bigger island, still no locks. Chains near the rim score triple and double. You need a chain that changes one rim tile without dragging another rim tile along.

⚓ Lock Harbour

3One lock

22 regions · par 1 swap · locks

A chain that joins two rim tiles is a lock (🔒): flipping it just moves the problem round the rim. One of Kempe's two classic swaps is locked here. Find the open one.

4Kempe's double

22 regions · par 2 swaps · locks

Both locks are shut: this is the case Kempe faced in 1879. His fix: two swaps on chains of the repeated element, one from each copy of it. Here that works. Par is 2.

5Heawood's catch

34 regions · par 2 swaps · locks

Doubly locked again, but now Kempe's double swap fails in both orders: the first swap reconnects the chain the second one needed. This is the gap Heawood found in 1890. Two swaps still do it; find another pair.

🌲 Far Chains Forest

6Far away

34 regions · par 2 swaps · locks

Every quickest solution starts with a chain that never touches the rim. Look away from the platform: change the island far out so that a rim swap opens up.

💡 π Loop Lighthouse

7.1Round and round

17 regions · locks · π

A small island with a loop in it. The rim is doubly locked, so the wheel marks one chain π. Flip π, then the new π, and so on: after 20 flips you are back here and the rim never opened. Some other chain leads off the loop. Par is 3.

7.2The loop

34 regions · par 3 swaps · locks · π

At a doubly locked rim one canonical chain is left, marked π on the wheel. Flip π, trace the new π, flip again: after 20 flips you are back where you started and the rim never opens. Every one of the 98 such loops in the order-34 census can be left by some other chain. Find it.

7.3Quiet way out

32 regions · par 2 swaps · locks · π

You are on a π loop again. This time every quickest way off it starts with a quiet chain: one that touches no rim tile at all. Look away from the platform first.

7.4Loud way out

31 regions · par 2 swaps · locks · π

Another loop, and the opposite lesson: no quiet chain helps here. The way off is a chain that touches the rim but is not π. Which rim chain breaks the rhythm?

7.5Rigid

32 regions · locks · π

The chain counter reads 8, the fewest possible: every chain on the island touches the rim. Only two different swaps change anything here, π and the swap that undoes the last π, and both keep the rim locked. Pick the direction that leads off the loop.

7.6Twin loops

34 regions · locks · π

This hole has two separate π loops, 20 flips each. You start on one of them. Get off without sliding onto the other.

89-8-9-8

34 regions · par 3 swaps · locks · π

The longest run of its kind in the 2.8 billion graphs of order 34: following π keeps the rim locked for 8 flips while the total chain count N goes 9, 8, 9, 8, … (watch the chain counter). Proving such a run can never close into a loop is an open problem (NRC). You don't have to follow π.

🧭 Census Frontier

17.1Eight seas

33 regions · par 3 swaps · locks · π

A 66666 hole: all five rim regions touch six others. Its colourings fall into 8 Kempe classes that no swap can cross, yet every class has colourings that fill. Of all of them, this is the only one 3 swaps from a fillable rim.

17.2Long locked shore

34 regions · par 3 swaps · locks · π

One of the five order-34 holes with the longest near-rigid run: following π keeps both rim locks shut for 8 flips with only 8 or 9 chains on the map. You start in one of its deepest colourings, 3 swaps from a fillable rim.

17.3Seven locked flips

34 regions · par 4 swaps · locks · π

The only hole among 3.6 million at order 34 where following π keeps both rim locks shut for 7 flips in a row. You start at the bottom of this map: 4 swaps from a fillable rim.

17.4Alone at four

34 regions · par 4 swaps · locks · π

A 75755 hole with an interior π-run of 6 from the order-34 census. Of its 6,857 colourings exactly one is 4 swaps from a fillable rim, and you are on it.

17.5Double record

34 regions · par 4 swaps · locks · π

A 66656 hole with two long runs at once: a near-rigid run of 8, the order-34 maximum, and an interior π-run of 5. You start in one of its deepest colourings, 4 swaps from a fillable rim.

17.6The order-33 record

33 regions · par 4 swaps · locks · π

The record hole of the order-33 census: the longest interior π-run (6 flips) among its 979,741 holes. Start 4 swaps out, at the bottom of this map.

17.7The eighth neighbour

🚧 Hurdle Cape

19.1Meet R*

34 regions · par 3 swaps · locks · π

This map is built round a piece the four-colour proof search got stuck on in October 2026. R* is a region with seven neighbours that no known reducible configuration could rule out. Here it sits next to the hole. Start 3 swaps out, at the bottom of this map.

19.2The degree-8 binder

33 regions · par 3 swaps · locks · π

Another piece the proof search got stuck on: a region with eight neighbours, with the degrees of its ring fixed exactly. The hole is one of that ring. You start in one of its deepest colourings, 3 swaps from a fillable rim.

19.3R* alone at four

19.4Binder pit
