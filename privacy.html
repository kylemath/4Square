Privacy

4square (4square.studio) · last updated 18 October 2026

4square is a puzzle game made by Kyle Mathewson. This page says what the game stores, what it sends, and who else is involved.

On your device

Your progress, coins, skins and settings are saved in your browser's local storage. Nothing about you is needed to play: there is no sign-up.

Gameplay research log

The game records how levels are played: the moves, hints and timings of each attempt, the outcome, and your device type, screen size and input type. It records no names, free text or location. Attempts are linked by a random id kept in your browser. On 4square.studio these records are uploaded to our Google Firebase database under an anonymous identity, to study how people solve these puzzles. You can switch this off on the account screen (🏆).

Accounts and leaderboards

If you join a leaderboard, the nickname you choose and your scores are stored in Firebase and shown to other players. If you sign in with Google, Firebase Authentication receives your Google account identity. You can delete your leaderboard entries from the account screen.

Advertising

The game may show ads served by Google AdSense. Google and its partners use cookies or similar technologies to serve ads based on your previous visits to this and other websites. You can turn off personalised ads at Google Ads Settings or opt out of some third-party vendors' cookies at aboutads.info. How Google uses data from sites that use its services is described at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites. Visitors in the EEA, UK and Switzerland are asked for consent before personalised ads are shown.

Children

The game is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them.

Contact

Questions or deletion requests: open an issue at github.com/kylemath/4Square.
