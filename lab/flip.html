Flip

  • Both polygons have the same numbered corners. Make your diagonals match the target's.
  • Tap a diagonal to flip it: it swaps to the other diagonal of its four-sided patch.
  • Each triangle has a sign, ＋ (sun) or − (sea). A diagonal can only flip when the triangles on both sides have the same sign, and then both new triangles take the other sign.
  • Green diagonals already match. Par is the fewest flips possible.
  • Free signs levels: before your first flip, tap triangles to choose their signs. A ✕ on the target means your signs can never reach that diagonal.

Why? Glue the two polygons along their rim and you get a map on a sphere. The signs are a secret 4-colouring of the corners (the colour button shows it): a flip is allowed exactly when the two far corners have different colours, and it never changes the colours. Shalom Eliahou conjectured that any two triangulations can be joined by signed flips for some choice of signs, and Gravier and Payan proved this is the same as the Four Colour Theorem.

Keys: Tab / arrows pick a diagonal, Enter flips, U undo, R reset, H hint, C colours, G sphere.
